Finer assumptions

Fixed in the rule

  • Rain wets a bench in any hour with at least 0.01 in, the smallest amount the station records.
  • Dew forms only while sunlight is under 10 W/m². Placeholder

About

The page counts only daytime hours (7am to 9pm unless you move them). A bench is too hot when the sun on it pushes it past a set temperature, wet after rain or dew until it has dried, and sittable when it is neither.

What it can't see

  • Bench materials and paint. Most are unrecorded; blank ones are assumed wood, and the rule treats every material alike.
  • Shade from anything but trees and woods mapped in OpenStreetMap ( trees). No buildings, and no leaves falling in autumn.
  • Puddles and drainage. Each bench is one surface.
  • Weather differences within the park. One station stands in for all of it.
  • People sitting on benches, which can warm or dry them.
  • How the sitter feels. Comfort also depends on air temperature and humidity, not just the bench.
  • New storms or dry spells. The 2050s keep 2025's rain and humidity; only temperature and dew point rise, so wetness barely changes.
  • Measured heating, drying and dew numbers. These are my assumptions.
  • Aug 29 7pm to Sep 2 noon, and two 3-hour stretches in September, when the station recorded nothing. Those hours are left out of every count, and benches start dry when the record resumes.
