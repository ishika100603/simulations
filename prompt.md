# Bench Heat and Wetness in Central Park

I am making a sandbox in the style of the course simmodeltwin.net. AGENTS.md explains what that means here: a web page with real data, a rule for applying it, a clock that runs the rule forward in time, and sliders that change the variables and assumptions. If AGENTS.md is missing from this session, please stop and direct the person running this to Tutorial 4. My sketch from Assignment 3 is in this folder as `Assignment3_Ishika_Agrawal.pdf`, and my Assignment 2 map of the park is `assignment2_map.png`. Please read both before anything else.

The question is simple. On a given hour between June and November, can you sit on a Central Park bench? In summer, a bench in full sun gets too hot to touch. In fall, rain and dew leave benches wet, and the shaded ones stay wet long after the open ones have dried. So shade is a gift in July and a nuisance in October. I want to follow every bench in the park through one season, hour by hour, and count how many of its daylight hours it was neither too hot nor wet. Then I want to run the same season again with the warmer summers expected in the 2050s, and see how many comfortable hours are lost and which benches lose them.

## The rule

Once the data is together, each bench gets a few fixed facts. It keeps its OpenStreetMap id and location, and its material, which I assume is wood wherever it's blank; I'm not using material in the rule this time, but I want it kept. A bench counts as under trees if it sits inside a wooded area, or if a tree is within the shade radius. That radius is my assumption, 25 feet by default, and it should be a slider from 10 to 50 feet, so please store each bench's distance to its nearest tree and let the page decide. The weather becomes one hourly table running from June 1 at midnight to November 30 at 11pm, 2025, in New York time, with air temperature, dew point, rain, wind and sunlight side by side.

Then, every hour, every bench is checked. First the sun: a bench in the open gets the full sunlight (GHI), and a bench under trees gets GHI × (1 − canopy blocking). I'm assuming trees block 80% of sunlight, as a slider from 0.5 to 0.95. Next its temperature: bench temperature in °F = air temperature + heating rate × (sun on the bench ÷ 100). My placeholder heating rate is 3.5°F per 100 W/m², which puts a bench in full midday sun (about 1,000 W/m²) 35°F above the air. That number is loosely based on Vanos et al.'s measurements of playground surfaces in sun and shade (2015, and 2022 in Building and Environment), and it should be a slider from 1 to 6. A bench is too hot when its temperature reaches 120°F, which I set below the levels Vanos found can burn skin; that threshold is also an assumption, as a slider from 100 to 140°F.

Wetness works like a small tank that fills and drains. A bench becomes fully wet (wetness = 1) in any hour with at least 0.01 inch of rain, the smallest amount the station records. It also gets wet from dew, at night, when the air is within the dew gap of its dew point: air temperature − dew point ≤ dew gap, while GHI is under 10 W/m². I've set the dew gap at 3.6°F (2°C) as a placeholder, borrowed from the plant science of "leaf wetness duration," which estimates how long leaves stay wet from ordinary weather station data (Sentelhas et al.). It should be a slider from 1 to 6°F. In every hour without rain or dew, the bench dries a little: wetness drops by (1 ÷ hours to dry) × (1 + wind in mph ÷ 20). Hours to dry depends on the sun on the bench. It's 2 hours in full sun (500 W/m² or more) and 8 hours in shade or at night (under 50 W/m²), interpolated in between. Both are placeholders, as sliders from 1 to 6 and from 4 to 16 hours. A bench is wet as long as its wetness is above zero.

A bench is sittable in any hour when it is neither too hot nor wet. Only daytime hours count toward the percentages, 7am to 9pm by default, as two sliders, since nobody sits on a bench at 3am. Wetness still has to be followed through the night, though, because a bench that was soaked at 3am may still be wet at 8am. ## The run

The run moves one hour at a time through the 4,392 hours from June 1 to November 30. The first version is just 2025, with the weather as it actually happened and nothing left to chance. The second is the 2050s: the same 2025 weather with a warming amount added to every hour's air temperature and dew point. I add it to the dew point as well so humidity stays about the same, which is an assumption. It means warming barely changes wetness in this model, and I'd rather say that openly in the limitations than hide it. Because the amount of warming is uncertain, the 2050s are run 100 times, each with a warming amount drawn from a triangular distribution between the NPCC4 low and high, peaking at the middle estimate. Please use a fixed random seed so the runs are the same every time the page opens. That's a lot of computing (477 benches × 4,392 hours × 100 runs), and the page has to stay responsive when a slider moves. Precompute whatever doesn't depend on a slider, and tell me your plan for the rest before you build it.

## The page

The page has the map in the middle, a panel on the left with the title, description, limitations and citations, and a panel on the right with the clock and the sliders. The map is the park drawn only from the OpenStreetMap context file, with no map tiles: the outline, paths and water, in the spirit of my Assignment 2 map and in the colors described below. Every bench is a dot. The page has two views. In the hour view, each dot shows its bench's state at the hour on the clock: ember orange if too hot, cyan-blue if wet, cream if sittable, and dimmed outside the counted hours. In the season view, each dot is split in half. The left half is colored by hours too hot in July (dim to ember orange) and the right half by hours wet in October (dim to cyan-blue), so you can see the July and October problems side by side on one bench. Hovering on a bench shows its row from the table. Under the map is a chart of the share of benches sittable each week from June to November: one line for 2025, and one for the 2050s with a band showing the range across the 100 runs. A thin vertical line follows the clock.

The controls follow the order in AGENTS.md. First comes the clock: a playhead from June 1 to November 30, hour by hour, with play, pause, fast forward and rewind, showing the date, hour, air temperature and whether it's raining. Then how it's drawn: hour view or season view, and 2025 or the 2050s. Then the main levers: the warming (showing the spread of the 100 runs, with a way to look at the low, middle and high cases), the too-hot threshold and the shade radius. Last come the finer assumptions: heating rate, canopy blocking, dew gap, hours to dry in sun and in shade, and the counted hours. Every slider that holds an assumption or placeholder should be quietly labeled as one, so a viewer knows it isn't measured.
